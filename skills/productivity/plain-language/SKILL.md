# Explain it in plain language

Explain the subject. Do not rewrite or edit files unless the user explicitly asks you to.

## Steps

1. Identify the topic or plan the user wants explained. Keep the audience and purpose they gave you. If they did not name an audience, write for a curious reader with no background in the subject.
2. Explain the main point and the facts the reader needs. For a topic, cover what it is, what it does, and why it matters. For a plan, explain its goal, actions in order, dependencies, and expected result. Do not add facts or promise results the source does not support.
3. Replace specialist terms with familiar words. Keep a technical name, command, or label only when the reader needs it to understand or act. Explain that name in everyday words the first time it appears. Leave out acronyms and details that do not help the reader understand.
4. Write directly. Prefer short sentences, concrete examples, and familiar comparisons only when they are accurate. Do not talk down to the reader or hide important limits, choices, or risks.
5. Check the explanation as someone new to the subject. Finish only when the topic's main point is clear or the plan's actions are clear and in order, important facts and risks remain, and no unexplained specialist term remains.
